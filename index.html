Learning the Autoregressive

Direct Score-Difference Estimation with Amortized Future Credit for Video Distillation

Leta fuses a directly-learned local correction with cross-chunk future credit into one teacher-guided update field, produced in a single estimator forward pass at generator update time.

Method

From a local gap to future-aware credit.

DMD reconstructs the full student score before subtracting it from the teacher's. Leta learns the gap itself, then lets the next chunk send credit back to the current one — both from a single shared estimator.

01 local correction

ScoreDelta

Rather than learning the student score and subtracting it from the teacher score, Leta regresses the teacher–student denoising residual directly. At the population optimum this recovers the same score difference DMD uses, up to a known timestep scaling; under a fixed-feature rank budget it can reach strictly lower approximation error when the residual is simpler to represent than the student denoiser.

update≈ ∇k

02 cross-chunk credit

FutureCredit

Detaching the generated history hides how the current chunk shapes everything after it, and backpropagating the full rollout costs too much memory. Leta replays the next chunk's final denoising step and takes a vector–Jacobian product against the next-chunk teacher residual, giving a learnable target that carries that influence backward.

update= ∇k + λ Ck

Abstract

Few-step distillation for autoregressive (AR) video generation commonly first initializes an AR student and then refines it with DMD. This refinement process faces two challenges. First, although initialization already yields a reasonably capable student, DMD obtains the refinement direction indirectly by estimating the full student score before forming the teacher–student correction. Second, detached-history training prevents the generator from assigning credit to a current chunk for its influence on subsequent chunks.

We introduce Leta, a few-step distillation framework that addresses these challenges with ScoreDelta and FutureCredit. ScoreDelta directly estimates the teacher–student correction from denoising residuals, while FutureCredit learns an auxiliary cross-chunk update signal from vector–Jacobian products (VJPs) of next-chunk teacher residuals. Leta combines these signals into a joint teacher-guided update field in a single forward pass during generator updates. Teacher evaluations and credit VJPs are used only for estimator training.

For ScoreDelta, we establish population equivalence to score-difference estimation and a conditional approximation advantage under limited capacity in a fixed-feature linear model. In our controlled experiments, Leta improves over DMD by 0.9 points on VBench and 0.66 on VideoAlign. This advantage persists at a higher generator update frequency (G:E = 1:1), where Leta improves over DMD by 1.6 on VBench and 0.35 on VideoAlign.
